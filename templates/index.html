What to watch

The bill count overstates policy coherence. The central challenge is reconciling overlapping and sometimes incompatible architectures across committees and vehicles.

Reporting regimes diverge

FRONTIER, the AI Incident Reporting Act, the AI Flaw Reporting bill, the Secure AI Development Act, and the intelligence authorization use different triggers, clocks, recipients, and mandatory or voluntary structures.

Evaluation is split across theories

Pending bills alternate between mandatory developer assurance, secure pre-release testing, federal evaluation capacity, federal-use testbeds, and voluntary documentation standards—with no clear reciprocity rule.

Preemption remains the fault line

FRONTIER’s targeted model differs sharply from the failed broad state-law moratorium. Any new amendment should be checked line by line for coverage of developers, deployers, generally applicable law, civil rights, privacy, minors, and procurement.

Model theft has a lifecycle gap

Prevention, detection, incident reporting, investigation, sanctions, and export-control response are spread across separate bills and agencies rather than joined into one statutory pathway.

CBRNE coverage is not end-to-end

Congress is addressing synthesis screening, model evaluation, terrorism assessment, and military incidents, but no single measure connects capability findings to controlled access, screening, escalation, and response.

Funding decides implementation

NIST, CAISI, CISA, NSA, Defense, and intelligence-community duties depend on appropriations. Authorization without people, compute, secure facilities, and evaluation funding may remain aspirational.

Methodology & maintenance

The site separates official source metadata from analyst-created classifications, preserves uncertainty, and treats every legislative change as a versioned event.

Inclusion and verification

  • Includes material safety/security duties, not every bill that mentions AI.
  • Open States supplies discovery candidates only.
  • Every published state record is checked against an official legislative or executive source.
  • Read the formal inclusion criteria.

Recommended operating cadence

  • Poll Congress.gov and GovInfo daily while Congress is active.
  • Check NDAA, IAA, appropriations, Rules, and committee pages several times per legislative day during markups and floor windows.
  • Human-review changes to categories, overlap analysis, and advancement tiers.
  • Run a monthly completeness sweep and an end-of-session audit.

Provenance model

  • Machine-derived: bill identity, sponsor, cosponsors, actions, referrals, text versions.
  • Analyst-derived: scope, primary type, committee dynamics, overlap notes, advancement tier.
  • Change log: store old value, new value, source URL, retrieval time, and reviewer.

Open data

Download the canonical dataset and JSON Schema. The source bundle also includes a validation script, a review-gated Congress.gov metadata refresher, and a scheduled GitHub Actions workflow.
